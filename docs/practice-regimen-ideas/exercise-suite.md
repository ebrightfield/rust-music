# Daily Guitar Practice — Exercise Suite Design

Status: **design proposal, v1**. Target: 30 min/day, one PDF per day, generated by an LLM
with tool access (Slonimsky CLI + LilyPond) and memory of recent days.

---

## 0. What this replaces, and why

A prior `practice-bot` ran 2026-05-12 → 2026-07-30 (~40 daily PDFs, in
`/home/eric/chaunacops/cllmi-bot-workspaces/practice-bot/`) and was cancelled. Reading those
artifacts, the failure modes were:

| Problem | Evidence | Fix in this design |
|---|---|---|
| **Sing-only, no guitar.** "No instrument" was printed on every sheet. | `morning_drill_*.pdf` header | Guitar in hands is the default; ear work is *integrated with* the instrument. |
| **Prose flood.** 5 pages of analysis paragraphs ("CT-I SATURATION: 50/33/50/50%") per sheet. | `2026-07-21` PDF | Hard budget: **≤1 line of prose per exercise**. The notation *is* the instruction. |
| **Analysis mistaken for practice.** The sheet explained the music instead of prescribing playing. | same | Every block states: what to play, how long, at what tempo, and one intention. |
| **No feedback loop.** `log.md` "Notes:" fields all `_(fill in)_`, never filled. | `skills/music/log.md` | One-line logging with a 3-field ritual, on the sheet itself, with a scannable checkbox. |
| **Narrow content.** Only major / mel-min / harm-min + rhythm. No chords, articulation, comping, improv. | 11 weeks of logs | Five fused blocks (§2) cover mechanics, ears, vocabulary, articulation, harmony/groove, and improv every day. |

Two things from the old system are worth keeping: **key rotation** and **seeded
reproducibility** (`--seed`) so a day can be regenerated identically.

## 1. Design principles (from `regimen_design.md`, restated as generator constraints)

These are your own principles; each becomes a rule the generator must obey.

**Rule 0 — Silver platter. The notation is the assignment.**
Everything playable is written out in staff + tab. The sheet never asks Eric to decide,
explore, choose, or figure out — because unnotated instructions don't get done. Banned from
every sheet: "explore…", "experiment with…", "take 30 seconds to decide…", "choose the
fingering that…", "try different…", "name it out loud". If a thing is worth practicing, it is
worth notating. Prose is limited to *what to play* and *one intention* — never *what to work
out*. This rule outranks every rule below it; where they conflict, notate.

**Rule 0b — Calibrate to a trained player.** Eric is a scholarship-level jazz guitarist
rebuilding chops, not a student. First drafts were far too easy: 2–4 bars per block, one
pentatonic box per sheet, 8ths at ♩=63, root-position voicings. Targets instead:
**8–16+ bars per block**, mixed subdivisions (sextuplets, 16ths, odd meters), irregular 7NS and
altered harmony, drop-2/drop-3 voicings, wide-interval patterns, positions up the neck. If a
block can be sight-read cold and dispatched in 30 seconds, it is wrong.

> **Fixed (example D).** The vocabulary block was 16 notes / 3 bars — no longer than the version
> that was called too easy — and papered the gap with "then up a m3", a *described* rather than
> notated transposition (a soft Rule 0 violation). Now written out in full: **84 notes / 12 bars**
> in three labelled sections — (a) all 7 degrees ascending, (b) the tuple negated `1,[-4,2,-3]`
> descending, (c) the same tuple down a m3 in A Lyd♭9♭13. Nothing is described that isn't
> notated. Current block note-counts: warmup 48, **vocabulary 84**, harmony 36, improv 26.

**Rule 0b-ii — No non-exercises. The content must be the hard part.**
An exercise fails if the *musical insight* is trivial even when the notation looks busy. Three
real rejections from review:

- **Parallel shape-shifting.** "Play C+ then D+ by sliding the same voicing up two frets" —
  correct, and worthless: whole-tone parallelism is the obvious reading, so there is nothing to
  practise. **Fixed** by finding the non-obvious application: C+ (C E A♭) is the altered V in
  **F, A and D♭** — a major 3rd apart — so one unmoving grip does three harmonic jobs while the
  chords around it change (`examples/w4.ly`).
- **Beginner comping.** Open-position G/Em/A/D with a Charleston stab. **Fixed** with drop-2
  voicings at frets 4–9, anticipations tied over the barline, and a chromatic approach chord
  (`examples/g4.ly`).
- **Unrealistic voicings.** `3-x-0-0-0-x` for G6 — what a chord-chart generator emits, not what a
  jazz player reaches for. **Rule: no open-string chords in comping blocks** (open strings are
  for deliberate colour, e.g. the Towner harvest, not for convenience).

Test before shipping a block: *state the insight in one sentence. If that sentence is obvious to
a trained player, the block is decoration.*

**Rule 0c — Terse mental cues are welcome; lectures are not.** One short line per block on
*approach* — looseness, breath, weight, thumb position, flow, "yes-and" — in the `intention:`
slot. Phrase them as cues, not paragraphs: "loose. no squeeze — check your thumb every 4 bars."
This is the one place non-notated text earns its keep, and it does *not* violate Rule 0 because
it describes *how to hold yourself*, not material to invent.

**Rule 0d — Print pitch-set relationships; don't explain them.** Subset/superset structure is
first-class content. Show it in set notation on the sheet — e.g.
`C Lyd♭9♭13 (7) ⊃ C△add♯11 (4) ⊃ C△♭5 (3) ⊃ ⟨C,G♭⟩` — and have each block play one rung of the
chain. `slonimsky subchords` / `superchords` / `contains` generate these.

**Rule 0e — Every sheet carries one harvested "idea of the day."** Systematic derivation
(combinatorics, tuples, enumerations) has a characteristic flavor and will *never* produce a
Towner open-string voicing, a Lage held-voice texture, or a McLaughlin open-string connector.
Those come from the **harvest library** (`harvest/`): externally-sourced devices from players of
interest, screened for notatability and cited. The harvest supplies one element of the day and
interlocks with the systematic ones like any other element. See `harvest/README.md` for the
screening test and attribution rules.

1. **Play phrases, not scales.** Every mechanical exercise gets a rhythm, a dynamic, and an
   articulation. A bare ascending scale is never an acceptable output.
2. **More notes to less.** Introduce a pitch set as a *subset of a larger one already in play*.
   7NS → 5NS → 4NC → 3NC → interval. Focus is on *choice under constraint*, not novelty.
   (Tooling: `slonimsky subchords --size N --name`.) The chain is **printed as notated lines**,
   not described.
3. **One in many keys vs many in one key.** Each day picks one axis. Never both — that's how a
   30-minute session turns into an inventory sweep.
4. **Not all 12 keys.** A rotation, not a sweep — a week sits in one or two keys. No weighting
   for now (all keys equal); the mechanism exists if priorities emerge later.
5. **Tempo ladders, never too fast to play.** Every timed block gets an explicit BPM, and
   ladders state their rungs. Ceiling = "clean at tempo", not max BPM.
6. **Musically synonymous fingerings: notate both, play both.** When a passage has ≥2 sane
   fingerings, print each as its own fully-fingered line and play them in sequence (a, b, a).
   The comparison happens through the hands, not through deliberation. *(Revised — the earlier
   version made choosing the exercise, which violates Rule 0.)*
7. **Listen before play.** At least one block audiates first. Made concrete: the target is
   notated, and the instruction is to sing it, then play it — not to "imagine something".
8. **Yes-and, with a model.** Open-ended blocks never say "don't", and never start from blank.
   A fully notated model line is given, plus a notated variation; free continuation comes
   *after* playing given material. See §2.1.
9. **Sustainability.** Warmup is mandatory, mezzo-piano, and relaxation-focused. Any block
   over ~4 min gets a shake-out. Pain = stop, and the sheet says so.
10. **Repertoire is out of scope for now.** No tunes, no chord-melody, no head-learning. The
    regimen targets mechanics, ears, vocabulary, articulation, rhythm, harmony, and improv.
    Songs fold in later, as a separate track.

## 2. Daily structure: five blocks, fused dimensions (30 min)

Articulation, vocabulary, rhythm, and ear are **dimensions**, not topics. Every exercise has an
articulation whether or not we name it; every exercise uses some pitch set. Giving each its own
box forced artificial separation and wasted page space. Instead: a few larger blocks, each
carrying several dimensions at once, with a **coverage matrix** (below) guaranteeing breadth
across the sheet.

| # | Block | Min | Primary | Fused dimensions |
|---|---|-----|---------|------------------|
| 1 | **Warmup** | 4 | mechanics, relaxation | articulation (legato/staccato), rhythm |
| 2 | **Hands + Vocabulary** | 7 | LH/RH mechanics on the day's pitch set | articulation, pitch-set, rhythm, position |
| 3 | **Ear → Hand** | 6 | audiate → sing → play the notated target | pitch-set, rhythm, articulation |
| 4 | **Harmony + Groove** | 7 | voicings, voice-leading, comping | rhythm/meter, articulation, dynamics |
| 5 | **Improv on a Model** | 6 | notated model → variation → free continuation | everything above |

Sum = 30, ±1 flex per block. Ordering: body → hands+ears → harmony → free play, so improv
lands in the warmed-up flow state.

### Coverage matrix (the breadth guarantee)

The generator must verify, per sheet, that each dimension is hit at least once. Because
dimensions are fused, one block often satisfies several — that's the point.

| Dimension | Satisfied by | Min/sheet |
|---|---|---|
| LH mechanics | blocks 1, 2 | 2 blocks |
| RH / picking (flatpick or fingerstyle) | blocks 1, 2, 4 | 2 blocks |
| Articulation (the day's technique) | ≥2 blocks, one of them musical | 2 |
| Pitch-set vocabulary | blocks 2, 3, 5 | 2 |
| Rhythm / meter / groove | blocks 1, 4 explicitly | 2 |
| Rest / phrasing / silence | blocks 4 or 5 (notated rests) | 1 |
| Ear (audiation, sing-then-play) | block 3 | 1 |
| Harmony / voice-leading | block 4 | 1 |
| Improvisation | block 5 | 1 |
| Dynamics | ≥2 blocks (explicit marks) | 2 |

### 2.1 How open-ended work stays silver-platter

Improv and audiation can't be fully notated without ceasing to be themselves. Resolution —
**notate a model, then vary it**, free play only after concrete material:

```
5 · IMPROV ON A MODEL — over Cm7 · F7 · Bb△     6 min · ♩=76
[ notated 4-bar line, staff + tab, fully fingered ]
   ↑ play as written, ×2
[ notated variation of bars 1–2 ]
   ↑ then this, ×2
then: keep going — same rhythm, your notes.
```

The written line *is* the prompt. Nothing on the page asks Eric to invent from nothing, and if
he only plays the notated material and stops, the block still had value. The same shape covers
audiation in block 3: the target phrase is notated, sung, then played — never "imagine a
melody".

## 3. Exercise families (the inventory)

Each family is a **parameterized generator**, not a fixed exercise. Parameters in `«…»`.
Families marked ⚙ have direct CLI support today; ✍ means LLM-authored LilyPond.

### 3.1 Mechanics (blocks 1–2) — from `pure_mechanics.md` + `LH Fingering Exercises.pdf`

Your 2012 "L.H. Fingering Sequences" sheet is already a generator: a basic pattern plus
variations (backwards, downwards, higher-fret, rotated, different-strings, repeating-notes).
Encode it directly.

| Family | Parameters | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **M1 Finger pairs** ✍ | pair ∈ {12,23,34,13,24,14}, string, position | The dyad drills from `pure_mechanics.md`. |
| **M2 Four-finger permutations** ✍ | perm ∈ 24 (6 classes), string set, direction | "24 reduces to 6 patterns you can rotate — but best not to think of them that way." Print the permutation literally. |
| **M3 Spiders** ✍ | anchor string (G/D), alternating pair above/below | Anchor two fingers, alternate the others. |
| **M4 Climbing** ✍ | pair {14, 13+24}, direction, span-contraction | Trailing finger contracts toward leading; hand migrates 1 fret/iteration. |
| **M5 Octave positions** ⚙ | note, CAGED grouping | `Octave Exercise.pdf`; note-rehearsal across the neck. |
| **M6 String crossing** ✍ | adjacency (adjacent / skip-1 / skip-2), pick pattern | The `pure_mechanics.md` "non-adjacent" extension. |
| **M7 RH picking** ✍ | alternate / economy / all-down / sweep; palm-mute on/off | Flatpick focus. |
| **M8 Fingerpicking** ✍ | p-i-m-a pattern, arpeggio figure | From `Fingerpicking Warmup&Etude.pdf`. |

Every M-family output carries: constant duration, **mp**, eyes-closed option, and a stated
relaxation cue. Extensions per the source doc: non-adjacent string switches, position shifts,
slides, bends.

### 3.2 Ear → Hand (block 3)

| Family | Parameters | CLI |
|---|---|---|
| **E1 Sight-sing** | key, scale, measures, difficulty | ⚙ `sight-reading` |
| **E2 Rhythm dictation / clap** | time-sig, style, syncopation, seed | ⚙ `rhythm-drill` |
| **E3 Audiate-then-find** | target interval / 3NC | ✍ sing first, then locate on neck without hunting |
| **E4 Call-and-response** | phrase length, pitch set | ✍ 2-bar given phrase, you answer 2 bars (yes-and) |

E1/E2 already have a working pipeline precedent. **E3–E5 are where "hear and express" lives**

### 3.3 Vocabulary — the combinatorics engine (block 2, woven into 3 & 5)

This is where your years of 3NC/4NC/5NS/7NS research plugs in, via `musical-combinatorics`
(`ThreeNoteChordQuality`, `FourNoteChordQuality`, `SevenNoteScaleQuality`) and the CLI.

| Family | Parameters | CLI |
|---|---|---|
| **V1 Melodic shapes in position** | pitch set, position, permutation order | ⚙ `practice-sheet`, `fretboard` |
| **V2 Chord-shape arpeggios** | pitch set, shape, up/down + down/up | ⚙ `chord-dictionary`, `voicings` |
| **V3 Dyads / interval pairs** | interval, pitch set, CAGED-ish shape | ✍ + ⚙ `fretboard` |
| **V4 Symmetrical scales** | chromatic / HW-dim / whole-tone / aug | ⚙ (few viable fingerings — that's the point) |
| **V5 Scale-pattern generator** | `(step, [series])` | ✍ **direct port of `Scale Patterns.pdf`** |
| **V6 Superset→subset drill** | 7NS → 5NS → 4NC → 3NC chain | ⚙ `subchords`, `superchords`, `contains` |

**V5 deserves emphasis**: your "Making Up Scale Patterns" sheet defines patterns as
`(scale-degrees-to-advance, [interval-series])` — e.g. `1,[2]`, `-1,[-2]`, `2,[0]`, `1,[1,1]`.
That is a compact, fully generative notation. The LLM picks a tuple, the sheet prints the
resulting line in notation + tab. Infinite non-repeating mechanical-but-musical material.

**V6 implements "more notes to less"**: pick the day's 7NS, derive a 5NS inside it, a 4NC
inside that, a 3NC inside that, an interval inside that. Play the chain in one position. This
is `regimen1.md`'s Part One→Three, made rigorous by `subchords`.

### 3.4 Articulation (a dimension, woven through blocks 1–5)

Not a standalone block. **One technique is the week's articulation focus**, and it appears
notated in at least two blocks per sheet — once mechanically, once musically. All are
expressible in LilyPond (verified: slurs, glissandi, bends, string indicators, accents,
up/down-bow, fermata, dynamics, parenthesized/ghost notes).

Rotation set: hammer-ons · pull-offs · slides (incl. ghosted/legato) · bends (½, whole,
release, pre-bend) · vibrato · tapping · palm muting · pick scrapes · harmonics ·
**pluck-position** (bridge↔neck timbre) · dynamics (terraced *and* graduated) · rakes/ghost
notes · dead/percussive strokes · thumb-vs-pick attack.

Two from `more_musical_exercises.md` seed this directly:
- 2-note-per-string Maj6 in 3/4 with root→3rd and 5th→6th slides, quarter notes, first note ghosted.
- Major-scale CAGED shapes, bend up / release down the highest note of each string.

### 3.5 Harmony + Groove (block 4)

| Family | Parameters | CLI |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 Voicing study** | chord, shape set, string group | ⚙ `chord-dictionary`, `voicings` |
| **H2 Voice-leading** | chord pair / progression | ⚙ `voice-leading`, `progression`, `annotate` |
| **H3 Progression comping** | changes, groove, strum/fingerstyle | ⚙ `analyze` + ✍ |
| **H4 Strum-pattern generator** | subdivision `s`, length `L` (2^L patterns) | ✍ **port of `Strum Patterns Generalized Approach.pdf`** |
| **H5 Melody over changes** | style/era, changes | ✍ **`narveson-melody` skill** |

Changes are **abstract progressions** (ii–V–I, turnarounds, modal vamps, blues), not tunes —
repertoire is out of scope (Rule 10). H4's strum patterns are **notated in full** with
down/up-stroke marks, never described as a rule to apply.

**H4** is another exact generator from your old sheet: pick smallest subdivision `s` and length
`L`; there are `2^L` patterns; down/upstroke assignment is mechanical (odd occurrences down,
even up), and rests are derived by cutting strums short. Fully enumerable, so the LLM can pick
*unpracticed* ones.

**H5** is where `narveson-melody` earns its keep: it authors *real melodies* — with shape,
direction, and phrase logic — over the week's changes, rather than patterns. Used pan-genre: the
skill's style profiles are a means to tonal variety, not a genre label to print on the sheet.

### 3.5b Harvested ideas (any block)

Not a block of its own — the day's harvest lands wherever it fits. Sources and screening live in
`harvest/README.md`; accepted records in `harvest/library.yaml` (10 records seeded: 9 documented,
1 rejected).

| Family | Parameters | Worked example |
|---|---|---|
| **X1 Mechanical device** ✍ | player, device, position | `examples/harv1.ly` — McLaughlin chromatic tetrachords with open-string connectors, D/U marks |
| **X2 Melodic procedure** ✍ | player, procedure, set | `examples/harv2a.ly` + `harv2b.ly` — Scofield 3NPS → 2NPS "faux pentatonic" (a/b pair) |
| **X3 Voicing concept** ✍ | player, concept | Towner: open strings woven into fretted voicings, wide spacing, sparse low register |
| **X4 Compositional technique** ✍ | player, technique | Lage: top-note-constant re-harmonisation (via Bach) |
| **X5 Stylistic trait** ✍ | player, trait | Lage pseudo-polyphony (held voice vs moving line); Scofield tritone/M7 double-stops |
| **X6 Mental cue** — | player, cue | Lage: "quietly. let the guitar do the work." → Rule 0c intention line |

**Scofield's faux-pentatonic (X2) is worth noting**: dropping one note per string from a 3NPS
fingering is *literally* rule 2 ("more notes to less") as a documented player device. The
harvest library and the combinatorics engine converge, which is a good sign for both.

### 3.6 Improv on a Model (block 5)

Structure is always **model → variation → free continuation** (§2.1). The notated model is
mandatory; the constraint is secondary and one line at most.

- **I1 Model line** ✍ — a fully notated, fully fingered 4-bar line over the day's changes,
  built from the week's pitch set and carrying the week's articulation. Often authored via the
  `narveson-melody` skill so it's a real melody with shape and direction, not a pattern.
- **I2 Notated variation** ✍ — bars 1–2 rewritten one way: rhythm displaced, contour inverted,
  articulation swapped, or a note substituted. Shows *how* to vary rather than saying "vary it".
- **I3 Free continuation** — one line: "keep going — same rhythm, your notes."

Constraint types (at most one per sheet, appended to I3): single pitch set · one string only ·
one position only · rhythm-motif-only · dynamics contour · articulation quota · rest-mandate
(silence ≥25% of bars) · target-note (land on the 3rd of each change) · groove-lock.

The **rest-mandate** and **groove-lock** constraints directly serve the "rhythmic intelligence:
groove, phrasing, rest, meter" emphasis. When a constraint is used, the *model line already
demonstrates it* — so the constraint is a description of what was just played, not a puzzle.

## 4. Repetition: weekly core + daily variation

Competence comes from repetition, so **the week is the unit, not the day.** One core of
material holds for seven days; exactly one knob turns each day. Same material, seven angles.

### Elements of the day (the breadth-with-focus rule)

"Focused" does **not** mean one thing per day — it means **one of each element**, chosen so they
interlock. This is the structural invariant that makes a sheet wide and coherent at once:

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Key | C |
| Position | VII–X |
| Scale / chord / pitch-set | Lydian ♭9 ♭13 (an irregular 7NS) |
| Melodic pattern | wide-leap fragment |
| Scale-pattern tuple | `1,[4,-2,3]` |
| Rhythmic idea | sextuplet 16ths; 5/8+7/8 |
| Articulation | slides + ghost notes |
| Right-hand technique | string skipping, economy picking |
| Dynamics | terraced *p*→*f* per phrase |
| Voicing | drop-2 with ♭9 on top |
| Voice-leading | that voicing's top voice descending by step |
| **Harvested idea** | McLaughlin open-string connector (`harvest/library.yaml`) |

**Synergy is the point, not a coincidence.** The voicing of the day should be *the* voicing used
in the voice-leading of the day, built from *the* pitch set of the day, in *the* position of the
day. One decision propagates. Where an element genuinely can't interlock (an ear exercise that
needs a plainer scale, say), bend the rule — it's a strong default, not a hard constraint.

Corollary for **warmups specifically: go wide, not narrow.** A warmup should generally use *all
four fingers, all six strings, and move across the fretboard* — not sit in one box on one
string. (My first drafts violated this: one string per bar, one position. Fixed — see
`examples/practice_example_D.pdf`.)

### The week's core (fixed Mon–Sun)

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| **Pitch-set chain** | Bb major (7NS) → Bb pentatonic (5NS) → Bbmaj7 (4NC) → Bb△ (3NC) → M3 |
| **Articulation focus** | slides (incl. ghosted) |
| **Scale-pattern tuple** | `1,[2]` (advance 1 degree, take 3rds) |
| **Changes** | Cm7 · F7 · Bb△ |
| **Improv model line** | one 4-bar notated line, reused all week |

### The daily knob (exactly one turns)

| Day | Knob turned | Everything else identical |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | position V, straight 8ths, flatpick | — |
| Tue | → position VII | same set, pattern, articulation |
| Wed | → 3/4 meter | back to position V |
| Thu | → new key (Eb), position V | same pattern + articulation |
| Fri | → swung 8ths | Eb retained |
| Sat | → fingerstyle | same everything else |
| Sun | → tempo ladder, integrative | replay the week's `hard`-flagged block |

Because only one variable moves, the sheet is recognizable on sight by Wednesday — that's the
competence mechanism. It also makes generation cheap and drift-resistant: the LLM re-emits the
week's core and applies one documented transform.

**Weekly rollover.** At week's end, advance the slower rotations one step each:
- **Key**: plain rotation, unweighted for now.
- **Articulation**: next technique in the §3.4 rotation.
- **Pitch-set family**: next family (diatonic → symmetrical → irregular → pentatonic …), chosen
  for tonal interest, not genre association.
- **Axis**: "one set in many keys" ↔ "many sets in one key" alternates weekly.
- **Right hand**: fingerstyle weeks ≥1 in 3.

Anything flagged `hard` twice in a week **carries into the next week's core** rather than
rotating out — the log's only job is to drive this.

## 5. Page layout (the anti-prose rule)

One PDF, **2–3 pages**, portrait. (Was "1–2"; honest volume per Rule 0b needs the room —
example D is 3 pages with pages 1–2 at ~80% fill, i.e. overflow from material, not padding.)
Per block:

```
┌ 2 · HANDS + VOCABULARY ───────────────── 7 min · ♩=72 ─┐
│ Bb major → pattern 1,[2] → pos. V, with slides         │  ← one line, says what to play
│ a) [ notation staff + tab, fully fingered ]            │
│ b) [ same line, alternate fingering, pos. VII ]        │  ← notate both, play a-b-a
│ intention: let the 4th finger stay soft.               │  ← one intention line
└────────────────────────────────────── ☐ done  ☐ hard ─┘
```

Header: date · day-of-week · week-number + day-knob · total time · key · RH mode.
Footer: the 3-field log ritual — **what felt free / what felt stuck / one thing to keep** —
plus per-block `done`/`hard` checkboxes. Short enough to actually fill in, unlike the old
`log.md`.

Toolchain: **LilyPond for all notation** (verified publication-quality: `treble_8` clef,
beaming, tab staff, articulations), **Slonimsky SVG for diagrams** (fretboards, pitch
circles), **Typst for page assembly** (already the pattern in `tools/drills_to_pdf.typ`).

### Verified toolchain notes

A working 2-page sample of the **five-block** design was built and visually verified; artifacts
in `sample/` (`sheet.typ`, `b1`–`b5b.ly`, `practice_2026-08-01.pdf`) and the fret helper in
`tools/fret2ly.py`. Findings, all confirmed by rendering:

1. **Slonimsky's notation SVG — was broken, now fixed (2026-08-03).** It previously rendered
   unbeamed eighths, missing barlines and dropped rests; those are fixed, along with two
   underlying engraver defects and a `treble-8` octave regression found on re-test. See
   `../slonimsky-cli-bugs.md`. **Current split:** Slonimsky for `rhythm-drill` /
   `sight-reading` blocks and all diagrams (`fretboard`, `pitch-circle`, `interval-matrix`);
   **LilyPond for anything needing tab** — warmups, vocabulary patterns, voicings, improv
   models — because Slonimsky has no tab staff yet. Note its clef errors still exit 0, so
   check output, not just exit status.
2. **Use LilyPond `--png -dresolution=200`, not `--svg`.** LilyPond's SVG emits tab fret
   numbers as `<text fill="currentColor">`; standalone, `currentColor` resolves to nothing and
   **every fret number renders invisible** in Typst — staves and note-dots appear, digits do
   not. PNG avoids it entirely. Always use `-dcrop` for tight bounding boxes.
3. **Set `\paper { line-width = … ragged-right = ##f }`** so systems stretch wide-and-short.
   Then `image(width: 100%)` in Typst is readable at music-stand distance. Sizing by height
   instead makes notation too small; sizing full-width without wide systems overflows the page.
4. **Always specify string numbers (`\5`, `\4`) for anything fretboard-specific.** Without
   them LilyPond picks its own strings, and bare `\relative` octave slips silently produce
   *unplayable* tab — the first drafts here generated frets 17–22 for a 5th-position warmup and
   frets 18–22 for comping voicings. Suppress the clutter on the staff with
   `\override StringNumber.stencil = ##f` while keeping tab correct.
5. **`subchords` labels sets relative to pitch-class 0, not the queried root** — asking for
   subsets of Bb major returns names like `Cmin7`. The generator must transpose labels back to
   the intended key before printing.
6. **Never hand-write LilyPond pitches for fretboard material. Use `tools/fret2ly.py`.**
   Author everything as `(string, fret)` pairs and convert. The octave-slip bug recurred in
   *three separate drafts* (frets 17–22 for a 5th-position warmup, 18–22 and 10–15 for
   voicings/lines) because absolute pitch names don't map intuitively to guitar register — and
   LilyPond compiles them happily. The helper also flags spans >4 frets and frets >15, and it
   caught two unplayable candidate fingerings during prototyping.
7. **Verify chord tones arithmetically, not by eye.** A "F7" voicing built by hand came out as
   C–G–A–E — no F, no Eb. Compute pitch classes from `(string, fret)` and compare against the
   intended chord before notating.
8. **Fingering markups must be 1–4.** Writing `-7` or `-8` (thinking "fret 7") renders a
   nonsense finger number. Fret info belongs in the tab staff; `-N` is always a finger.
9. **Count every bar to the meter.** Hand-authored rhythms silently drifted into dotted values
   that didn't sum to 4/4. Total the durations per bar before compiling.
10. **Verify chord *symbols* against the voicing, not just playability.** Hand-written labels
    were wrong three times: a "F7" that was C–G–A–E, a "G6" that was a bare G triad, an "Em7"
    missing its 7th, and a "D7" in a whole-tone context that was really D augmented. Compute
    the pitch classes and compare to the symbol before printing either.
11. **True bend notation is not available in LilyPond 2.26 via `\bendOn`** (unknown command) and
    `\glissando` + `Bend_spanner_engraver` + `supportNonIntegerFret` still renders a slide.
    `\bendAfter` is a fall-off, not a bend-up. **Use chart convention instead**: a slur plus a
    `^\markup { \teeny "full" }` / `"½"` label above the target note. Verified legible.
12. **Escape `#` in Typst body text** (`F\#`), or Typst parses it as code and errors.
13. **String numbers alone don't pin neck position — use `\set TabStaff.minimumFret`.**
    Given `\5`, LilyPond still picks the *lowest* playable fret on that string, so an
    octave/neck-mapping exercise collapsed to frets 3-3-1-1 instead of walking 8→3→10→5→13→8.
    Set `minimumFret` (plus `restrainOpenStrings = ##t`) per position change. This is how
    M5 (octave positions) and any CAGED-position exercise must be written.
14. **Typst can't import above its compile root** — `#import "../tools/sheet.typ"` fails with
    "would escape the project root". Keep `sheet.typ` beside the day's `.typ`, or compile with
    `--root`.
15. **Liberation Sans silently drops ♭ (U+266D), △ (U+25B3), ⊓/⊔ (U+2293/4).** No warning —
    the glyphs render as *blank*, so "C Lydian ♭9♭13" printed as "C Lydian 9 13" and example C's
    "⊓ down, ⊔ up" stroke legend was invisible. `tools/sheet.typ` now sets a fallback list
    (`Liberation Sans`, `Noto Sans Symbols`, `Noto Music`); Typst falls through per-glyph.
    Note `♯` and `♩` *are* covered, which is why the loss was easy to miss.
16. **A tall `breakable: false` Typst block silently wastes a whole page.** When the expanded
    vocabulary block no longer fit in the remainder of page 1, it jumped wholesale to page 2 and
    left page 1 **50% blank** — which reads as padding. Two fixes, both needed: pass
    `breakable: true` on tall blocks, *and* split long notation into several images (one per
    musical section), since a single image is atomic and cannot break. After both, pages 1–2
    fill 83%/79%.
17. **LilyPond identifiers cannot contain digits.** `nps3 = { … }` fails with
    "syntax error, unexpected UNSIGNED"; use `npsThree`.
18. **A 7-note pattern rarely lands on a bar boundary.** 7 degrees × 4-note fragments = 28
    eighths = 3.5 bars, so every section ended mid-bar and LilyPond emitted "bar check failed".
    Pad each section to a whole number of bars (a trailing `r2` also gives useful breathing room
    between sections).
19. **Check that a transposition still fits the neck.** Transposing this pattern *up* a m3 ran
    past fret 15 and became unplayable; *down* a m3 (A Lyd♭9♭13) sits in frets 5–12. Verify
    placement before committing to a direction.
20. **Verify by rendering, never by exit code.** Every defect above compiled "successfully".

## 6. Generation pipeline (per day)

Two modes: **week-start** (author a new core) and **mid-week** (re-emit core, turn one knob).
Mid-week is the common case and is deliberately cheap.

1. **Read state**: the current `week.yaml` (core material + which knob each day turns), plus
   recent daily manifests and anything flagged `hard`.
2. **Decide**:
   - *Week-start*: pitch-set chain, articulation focus, pattern tuple, changes, improv model
     line, key. Carry over anything `hard`-flagged twice last week.
   - *Mid-week*: no new content — apply the day's single documented transform.
3. **Query tools**: `subchords`/`superchords`/`contains` for the set chain, `chord-dictionary`/
   `voicings` for shapes, `progression`/`voice-leading` for changes, `rhythm-drill`/
   `sight-reading` (`--seed`) for ear material. Transpose `subchords` labels to the real root.
4. **Author** LilyPond per block — staff + tab, **explicit string numbers**, fully fingered.
   Compile with `--png -dresolution=200 -dcrop`.
5. **Assemble** with Typst → `practice_YYYY-MM-DD.pdf`.
6. **Write manifest** (`YYYY-MM-DD.yaml`): every decision + seeds, so the day is reproducible
   and tomorrow's run has memory.
7. **Verify** — hard gates, all mechanical:
   - compile succeeded **and pages rendered to PNG were inspected** (not just exit code);
   - page count ≤3 **and every page before the last ≥70% full** (catches the
     `breakable: false` trap below, where a tall block jumps a page and leaves it half blank);
     minutes sum to 30; all five blocks present;
   - coverage matrix (§2) satisfied;
   - **every playable thing is notated** — no block whose only content is prose;
   - **banned-phrase scan**: reject on "explore", "experiment", "try different", "decide",
     "choose", "figure out", "come up with", "your choice" (Rule 0);
   - **playability check**: all tab frets within 0–15 and span ≤4 per position — catches the
     octave-slip bug that silently produced frets 17–22.

Step 7 is the guard against both failure modes: the old system's page-bloat drift, and the
silent-unplayable-notation bug found while prototyping.

## 7. Decisions taken

- **Blocks are fused, not siloed.** Articulation and vocabulary are dimensions carried by other
  exercises; breadth is enforced by the coverage matrix (§2), not by one box per topic.
- **Silver platter is Rule 0** and outranks everything. Notated material always; no
  explore/decide/choose instructions. Enforced mechanically by a banned-phrase scan.
- **Open-ended work gets a notated model** — model → variation → free continuation (§2.1).
  Inherently generative work stays generative; structure is added wherever it can be.
- **Repetition is weekly-core + one daily knob** (§4).
- **Two fingerings → notate both, play both** (a-b-a). No deliberation.
- **Repertoire is out of scope for now.**
- **Duration: 30 min** for now, to be tuned by live testing.
- **Keys: unweighted rotation** for now.
- **Reading vs memory: mixed, per exercise.** Not a global setting. Warmup and mechanical blocks
  are memorizable within a day and should end up eyes-closed; notated lines (improv models,
  ear targets, comping voicings) are read first and drift toward memory as the week repeats
  them. Since the week's core is fixed (§4), *reading naturally converts to memory by midweek*
  without the sheet having to say so.
- **Pan-genre, framed as "units of work".** No genre-of-the-day. Material is chosen for tonal
  interest and exploratory value, not to represent a style. A bossa clave or a swung line may
  appear because the *rhythmic unit* is worth practicing, never because it is Saturday's genre.
  Genre labels stay off the sheet.

## 8. Remaining open question

**Live-test feedback loop.** Everything above is designed but unvalidated against actual
playing. The one thing the design can't settle on paper is whether 30 min of five blocks feels
like too much material per sheet — the fused-block change cut the block count from six to five,
but block 2 (7 min, two fingerings, a-b-a) may still be dense. Worth watching in the first week
and cutting block minutes before cutting blocks.
